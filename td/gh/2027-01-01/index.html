Human Growth Hormone (hGH) Isoforms Differential Immunoassays for Doping Control Analyses

1.0 Introduction

The purpose of this Technical Document (TD), which constitutes an integral part of the International Standard for Laboratories (ISL[1]), is to ensure a harmonized approach in the application of the Isoform Differential Immunoassays for the detection of Use of human Growth Hormone (hGH) in sport. This ISL TD provides direction on the Sample pre-analytical preparation procedure, the performance of the Test Method, and the interpretation of the test results.

The Isoforms Differential Immunoassays for the detection of doping with hGH were developed to distinguish between the proportions of hGH isoforms found under normal physiological conditions and those found after recombinant (Rec) hGH injection[2, 3]. The Test Method is essentially based on the established principle that the normal composition of hGH in blood is a mixture of different isoforms, present at constant relative proportions in an individual. In contrast, Rec GH is comprised almost exclusively of the monomeric 22-kDa molecular form. Thus, the Use of Rec hGH not only leads to an increase in the concentration of the 22-kDa isoform but also causes a reduction of the non-22-kDa concentrations, thus altering the natural ratios established between these hGH isoforms[4].

1.1 Principle of the Test Method

Two (2) separate kits (Kit '1' and Kit '2', supplied by CMZ-Assay GmbH, Germany) are used for the measurement of the hGH isoforms in Samples[5].

Each kit contains one 'recombinant' (Rec) and one 'pituitary' (Pit) assay. In the Rec assay, the coated capture antibody preferentially binds to the monomeric 22-kDa hGH present in the Samples, whereas the Pit assay employs a capture antibody that recognizes a variety of pituitary-derived hGH isoforms. The respective assays are referred to as "Rec1", "Pit1", "Rec₂" and "Pit2". The result of the Test Method is expressed as the ratio of the concentrations Rec/Pit for each particular kit. Elevated Rec/Pit ratios are utilized to indicate doping with Rec hGH.

The initial version of the Isoforms Differential Immunoassays was based on a coated tube (CT) platform. In 2026, the assays were transitioned to a microtiter plate (MTP) format. Apart from this change in assay platform, the reduction of serum Aliquot volume from 50 µL/tube to 25 µL/well, and the corresponding use of an appropriate MTP luminometer, the assays remain essentially the same, using the same capture and detection antibodies, reagents and detection system.

2.0 Assay Requirements

Prior to the implementation of this Test Method in Doping Control analysis, the Laboratory shall fulfill the following requisites:

2.1 Test Method Validation Requirements

Validate the assay performance on-site, including:

a) Estimation of the Limit of Quantification (LOQ), relative within-Laboratory Repeatability (sr, %) and relative Intermediate Precision (sw, %) of Rec and Pit concentrations (Rec₁; Pit₁; Rec₂; Pit₂).

[Comment to Article 2.1.a: the LOQ of Rec and Pit determinations is defined as the lowest measurable concentration with sr <15% and sw < 20%.]

b) Estimation of the combined relative standard Measurement Uncertainty (uc, %) of the concentration ratios (Rec₁/Pit₁; Rec2/Pit₂) at levels close to the corresponding Decision Limits (DLs) - see Article 4.0.

c) Verification of the suitability of use of the same (one) calibration curve for different plates of the same kit lot analyzed the same day (if needed).

2.2 Test Method Accreditation Requirements

a) Participate successfully in at least one WADA-organized EQAS in order to demonstrate readiness for assay implementation. In cases of identified deficiencies, proper corrective action(s) shall be implemented.

b) Obtain ISO/IEC 17025 accreditation for the hGH Isoforms Differential Immunoassays method from an Accreditation Body that is a full member of the Global Accreditation Cooperation Inc. and a signatory to the Mutual Recognition Agreement (MRA) of the Global Accreditation Cooperation Inc.

2.3 Assay Pre-Analytical Procedure

Upon reception of the "A" and "B" Samples at the Laboratory, the following steps shall be followed:

a) Check that the whole blood Samples have been collected in tubes containing an inert polymeric serum separator gel and a clotting activation factor (e.g. BD Vacutainer ® SSTTM -II tubes, EU ref 367955; BD Vacutainer ® SSTTM -II Plus Advance tubes, EU ref 367954; BD Vacutainer ® SSTTM tubes, US ref 367986) in accordance with WADA's International Standard for Testing (IST)[7]. Check that the Samples have been kept refrigerated (shall not have been frozen) following collection and during transportation to the Laboratory.

b) Alternatively, serum Samples may be received at the Laboratory, frozen or refrigerated, following the clotting and centrifugation of the collected whole blood Samples and separation of the serum fraction at the site of Sample collection.

c) Any Samples delivered to the Laboratory as plasma shall not be accepted for the purposes of hGH Isoforms Differential Immunoassay analysis. The Laboratory shall notify and seek advice from the Testing Authority (TA) regarding rejection or Analytical Testing of Samples for which irregularities are noted (see International Standard for Laboratories, ISL[1]). In cases of Sample collection in the incorrect matrix, the results of the analysis shall be disregarded.

d) Check the status of the Sample(s) and the integrity of the collection tubes (for example, evidence of breakage of the separating gel). The Laboratory shall note any unusual condition of the Sample, record such condition(s) and include it in the Test Report in ADAMS.

2.4 Analytical Testing Procedure

a) For the performance of the Analytical Testing Procedure (ATP), refer to the test procedure described in the Instructional Insert provided with the test kits and the Laboratory's Standard Operating Procedure (SOP).

b) In cases of contradiction between the Instructional Insert provided with the kits and the Laboratory's SOP, or between the Instructional Insert and this ISL TD, the latter document shall prevail in each case.

p. 6

c) For the production of test results, the Laboratory shall use the Berthold LB 963 Centro "WADA Edition" microtiter plate (MTP) luminometer (Berthold Technologies GmbH, Germany), or another instrument approved by WADA.

2.4.1 Analytical Testing Strategy

a) Initial Testing Procedure

i. Either Kit '1' or Kit '2' may be used for the ITP using at least two (2) Aliquots taken from the original "A" Sample.

ii. Each ITP analysis should include the use of the Kits' positive (control 2) and negative (control 1) Quality Control (QC) samples. In addition, or alternatively, the Laboratory may use internal Quality Control (iQC) samples, prepared at an approximate Rec/Pit ratio = 1.50 - 2.50 (see Article 4.1 c).

If the Laboratory's iQCs are used, their performance shall be followed using QC-charts. It is recommended to use iQCs regularly in the ITP to enable the collection of sufficient data to build the QC-Charts.

b) Confirmation Procedure

i. Both Kit '1' and Kit '2' shall be used for the CPs ("A" and "B", where applicable)

ii. Three (3) Sample Aliquots taken from the respective original "A" or "B" Sample shall be measured, except in cases of limited Sample volume, in which case the maximum number of Aliquots that can be prepared should be analyzed[1].

iii. The Laboratory shall define in the SOP the circumstances where the CP shall be repeated (e.g., values of intra-assay sᵣ ≥ 15%).

iv. The Laboratory shall use well-characterized and stable iQC samples, which are not subject to kit lot variations, for the verification of CP performance. The selection of the iQC sample(s) to be used shall be based on the results of the ITP (e.g., iQC with closest Rec concentration to that measured for the Sample in the ITP) - see Article 4.1 c).

[Comment to Article 2.4.1: The performance of new kit lots shall be verified through the measurement of the Laboratory's iQC(s) and their evaluation using QC-charts. Deviation of measured Rec and Pit concentrations of more than (>) 20% between lots shall trigger an internal investigation and, if it is confirmed that this is due to an impaired performance of the new kit lot, the Laboratory shall inform WADA immediately. WADA will then prompt an investigation by the kits' manufacturer.]

3.0 Interpretation and Reporting of Test Results

3.1 Interpretation of Test Results

For determination of compliance of the analytical result (i.e., the Rec/Pit ratio), the Laboratory shall:

a) Calculate the mean concentrations for the Rec and Pit assays from the replicate analyses of the Sample Aliquots. Truncate the calculated mean concentrations (ng/mL) to three (3) decimal places and use these truncated values to calculate the respective Rec/Pit ratio.

For Samples with mean Pit concentrations below the Laboratory's validated assay LOQ (Pit < LOQPit), utilize the respective LOQPit (expressed to three (3) decimal places) to calculate the Rec/Pit ratio.

b) Compare the calculated Rec/Pit ratio (expressed truncated to two (2) decimal places) with the corresponding DL for males or females, as applicable, established for the test kit used[8]. The DL values to be used are:

Kit '1': Males (1.84); Females (1.63)

Kit '2': Males (1.91); Females (1.59)

3.2 Reporting of Test Results

When reporting an AAF or an ATF for hGH, which is a Threshold Substance, the Laboratory Test Report shall include:

a) The Rec/Pit ratios obtained during the CP for each kit (expressed truncated to two (2) decimal places).

Where the mean measured Pit concentration is < LOQPit, and the corresponding Laboratory-validated LOQPit is used for calculating the Rec/Pit ratio, the result shall be reported as "greater than" (>) (e.g. if Rec = 0.200 ng/mL and the Laboratory's LOQPit = 0.050 ng/mL, the Rec/Pit ratio shall be reported as "> 4.00");

b) The uc (%) of the Rec/Pit ratios at values close to the corresponding DLs as determined by the Laboratory during Test Method validation (expressed truncated to two (2) decimal places).

c) The values of the applicable sex- and kit-specific DLs.

d) The mean Rec and Pit concentrations for each kit calculated from the triplicate determinations and expressed in ng/mL truncated to three (3) decimal places.

In addition, the Laboratory Documentation Package (LDOC) shall also include the Rec and Pit concentrations, expressed in ng/mL truncated to three (3) decimal places, for the three (3) Sample Aliquots analyzed using Kit-1 and Kit-2.

[Comment to Article 3.2: The decision rule applicable to CPs used for the analysis of endogenous Threshold Substances, for which the Threshold value(s) have been established based on reference population statistics, already incorporates a guard band that reflects the uncertainty of the measurements associated with the Quantitative Procedure. Therefore, the zone of analytical values considered compliant (Negative Finding) or not (AAF, ATF) with this decision rule would be defined by the Threshold value itself, which constitutes the DL. The assay Measurement Uncertainty (MU, expressed as uc) shall not be added to the test result for reporting an AAF or an ATF.]

Test Report Example (for a Sample from a male Athlete):

The analysis of the Sample using the hGH Isoforms Differential Immunoassays has produced the following results for the concentrations: Rec₁ = 5.032 ng/mL, Pit₁ = 1.996 ng/mL, Rec₂ = 4.895 ng/mL, Pit₂ = 2.039 ng/mL, and ratios Rec₁/Pit₁ = 2.52 and Rec₂/Pit₂ = 2.40, which are greater than the corresponding DLs of 1.84 and 1.91, respectively. The combined relative standard uncertainty (uc, %) estimated by the Laboratory at levels close to the DL is 11.90 % for Kit '1' and 10.53 % for Kit '2'. This constitutes an AAF for hGH.

4.0 Estimation of Assay Measurement Uncertainty

4.1 Relative Combined Standard Uncertainty (uc, %)

a) The Laboratory shall estimate the assays' uc (%) using an approach consistent with the principles described in the ISO/IEC Guide to the Expression of Uncertainty in Measurement (GUM)[9]. A 'top-down' approach, based on the estimation of assay sᵣ, sw and Bias (B), is recommended.

b) The Laboratory shall generally refer to the ISL TD DL[10] for the estimation of assay MU.

c) Use of Internal QCs:

For estimation of uc (%), the Laboratory shall prepare iQC samples at different concentration levels of Rec and Pit (at least three (3) dilutions) to yield an approximate Rec/Pit ratio = 1.50 - 2.50. This would ensure that the uc (%) is estimated over the working range of the assays and at levels close to the DLs. The iQCs should be measured at least in triplicate (x3) over 6 days under reproducibility conditions.

4.2 Maximum Allowed Levels of Measurement Uncertainty: uc _Max (%)

The Laboratory shall have values of uc (%) for the Rec/Pit ratio determinations, estimated during Test Method validation at values close to the DL, which are not higher than (≤) 20%.

4.3 Verification of Measurement Uncertainty

The Laboratory shall refer to the ISL TD DL[10] for on-going verification of the assay uc (%) estimates.

5.0 Detection of Synthetic Long-acting GH Analogues

In addition to preparations of recombinant 22-kDa hGH, several synthetic preparations of Long-Acting GH Analogues (LAGH) comprising different rhGH variants (e.g., conjugates, fusion proteins) exist.

Some of these LAGH include:

a) Eftansomatropin-α (GX-H9, Genexine): Recombinant fusion glycoprotein of 22-kDa hGH and human C-terminal hybrid Fc (hyFc ® ) domain with sequences of IgD and IgG4.

b) Redalsomatropin-α (JR-142, JCR Pharmaceuticals): Recombinant fusion glycoprotein of modified HSA (585 amino acids) attached to the C-terminus of 22-kDa hGH.

c) Somapacitan (Sogroya ® , NovoNordisk): 22-kDa hGH (single aminoacidic substitution at position L101C) conjugated through a fatty acid linker to human serum albumin (HSA).

d) Somatrogon (MOD-4023, Pfizer): Recombinant fusion glycoprotein of 22-kDa hGH and three (3) copies of the C-terminal peptide of the β-subunit of human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG).

p. 10

Due to the structural difference of these LAGH preparations, their detection with specific Liquid Chromatographic - Mass Spectrometric (LC-MS) Test Methods[11-14] is recommended.

It is also worth noting that the hGH Isoforms Differential Immunoassays Kit '2' may also serve as an ITP for some of these preparations (e.g., Somatrogon[12,15], Redalsomatropin-α[13]). Therefore, when there is a PAAF using Kit '2' for the ITP, which is not confirmed with Kit '1' during the CP, the Laboratory shall consider performing (or subcontracting to another Laboratory, in consultation and after approval by the TA) the LC-MS analysis as a CP for the detection of LAGH.
