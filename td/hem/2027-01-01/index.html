Analytical and Reporting Requirements for the Blood Markers of the Hematological Module of the Athlete Biological Passport

1.0 Introduction

The purpose of this Technical Document (TD), which constitutes an integral part of the International Standard for Laboratories (ISL)[1], is to harmonize the analysis and reporting of whole blood Samples collected for the measurement of individual Athlete blood Markers within the framework of the Hematological Module of the Athlete Biological Passport (ABP). The analysis is performed by Laboratories and ABP Laboratories (i.e., Laboratories), which have the Test Method incorporated into their ISO/IEC 17025 or ISO 15189 Scope of Accreditation, as applicable.

[Comment to Article 1.0: If it is not reasonably possible for whole blood Samples to be analyzed in a Laboratory for technical and/or geographical reasons, the Samples can be analyzed at a satellite facility of a Laboratory or using mobile units operated by a Laboratory under their applicable accreditation (ISO/IEC 17025 or ISO 15189). Satellite facilities and mobile units shall also be (ISO/IEC 17025 or ISO 15189) accredited and participate successfully in the applicable WADA External Quality Assessment Scheme (EQAS) for the Hematological Markers of the ABP prior to the analysis of whole blood Samples, as determined by WADA.]

2.0 Whole Blood Sample Reception and Timing of Analysis

a) Sample handling shall be conducted in compliance with the ISL[1] and the ISL TD on Laboratory Chain of Custody (ISL TD LCOC)[2].

b) The Laboratory may receive whole blood Samples as an "A" Sample only or as "A" and "B" Samples, which have been collected in approved tubes, sealed, and transported to the Laboratory in compliance with the International Standard for Testing (IST)[3].

c) A whole blood Sample collected for the analysis of the hematological Markers of the ABP shall be analyzed as soon as possible, and:

i) No later than twelve (12) hours after reception at the Laboratory, or

ii) Beyond twelve (12) hours after reception if the Sample Collection Authority (SCA) provides information regarding the Sample collection and transportation conditions, which projects that the Blood Stability Score (BSS) will be acceptable and should not compromise the validity of the Sample.

d) The Laboratory is responsible for maintaining the whole blood Sample(s) at a cool temperature (approximately 4°C) between reception and the start of homogenization, if unable to analyze the whole blood Sample immediately after reception.

e) The temperature data logger included in the Sample transportation package shall accompany the whole blood Sample(s) until the start of the homogenization procedure.

p. 2

f) The whole blood Sample shall not be aliquoted for the performance of other Analytical Testing Procedures (ATPs) [e.g., Homologous Blood Transfusion (HBT), Erythropoiesis Receptor Agonists (ERAs)] until the analysis of the blood Markers of the Hematological Module of the ABP is satisfactorily conducted. Only after the analysis has been satisfactorily completed may the Laboratory aliquot the whole blood Sample for the performance of other ATPs.

g) If there are irregularities observed with a whole blood Sample, the Laboratory shall proceed with the analysis [as per ISL Article 5.3.2.1 c) - ii] [1] to ensure that the analysis is conducted within an acceptable BSS and report the results into ADAMS with a detailed description of the deviation.

h) If the whole blood Sample cannot be analyzed, the Laboratory shall report the Sample as "Not Analyzed" and document the condition(s) that prevented the analysis in the Sample's ADAMS record.

3.0 Instrument Performance and Quality Control

3.1 Instrument

a) Whole blood Samples shall only be analyzed in Laboratories, with instrumentation having compatible technical characteristics, as determined by WADA.

b) The Laboratory shall maintain the instrument as per ISL Article 5.2.4[1].

c) The Laboratory shall ensure that the operational parameters of the instrument and associated reagents comply with the manufacturer's specifications before performing an analysis.

3.2 Quality Control

As part of the Laboratory's Quality Control activities to ensure the validity of the analytical results, the Laboratory shall:

a) Analyze the Quality Control (QC) samples (e.g., XN CHECK QCs) furnished exclusively by the instrument manufacturer, as determined by WADA, and handled in strict accordance with the manufacturer specifications (e.g., expiration dates, storage conditions).

b) Ensure that the appropriate lot number of the QC samples (e.g., XN CHECK QCs), as determined by WADA, are received through the relevant local distributor and analyzed within the relevant start and expiration dates.

c) Ensure the analytical instrument(s)' capability to connect to and automatically upload the manufacturer's QC data into the relevant instrument manufacturer's reporting platform (e.g., CaresphereTM XQC). The Laboratory should contact WADA if there are issues with connectivity.

p. 3

d) Download the monthly instrument manufacturer's report (e.g., CaresphereTM XQC) for their records. These reports shall be stored in the Laboratory's files for a period of ten (10) years and provided to WADA upon request.

e) Analyze the QC samples at least once a week during routine Laboratory operational periods to monitor instrument performance if no routine analyses have been performed and the instrument has remained idle.

3.3 Fresh Whole Blood Analysis

At least four (4) times a year, on a quarterly basis, a fresh whole blood sample shall be analyzed. Whole blood Samples collected from Athletes shall not be used for this analysis.

[Comment to Article 3.3: It is recommended that the analysis of a fresh whole blood sample is conducted during the WADA EQAS round for the Hematological Module of the ABP, prior to the analysis and reporting of the EQAS results (e.g., between the EQAS shipment date and reporting deadline).]

a) QC samples (all levels), as described in Article 3.2, shall be satisfactorily analyzed prior to the fresh blood sample repeatability analysis.

b) The fresh whole blood sample shall be gently homogenized for at least fifteen (15) minutes on an appropriate mixer (e.g., roller mixer).

c) The fresh whole blood sample shall be analyzed at least seven (7) consecutive times. The repeatability of the determinations, expressed as coefficients of variation (CV %), shall be assessed using the following criteria:

i) below (<) 1.5% for Hemoglobin (HGB) and Hematocrit (HCT), and

ii) below (<) 15% for Reticulocyte percentage (RET%).

4.0 External Quality Assessment Scheme

a) Laboratories shall participate in, and satisfactorily meet the requirements of, WADA's EQAS [4, 5] for the blood Markers of the Hematological Module of the ABP to apply the ATP to whole blood Samples.

b) Each instrument utilized in the analysis of whole blood Samples in support of the Hematological Module of the ABP shall be included in the WADA EQAS.

c) Prior to the analysis of EQAS samples, all QC samples (e.g., XN CHECK) shall be analyzed in duplicate following the specifications provided by the manufacturer. The results for all QC samples shall be in agreement with the relevant ranges established by the manufacturer.

d) EQAS samples shall be handled and prepared for analysis as per the EQAS provider's instructions.

e) The EQAS samples shall be analyzed and reported as instructed by the EQAS provider.

p. 4

f) The Laboratory shall download and store the raw temperature profile report recorded by the temperature data logger, if included with the EQAS samples shipment.

g) WADA's EQAS program is the only EQAS relevant to the Laboratory's compliance with the requirements for the analysis of the blood Markers of the Hematological Module of the ABP.

5.0 Analysis of Whole Blood Samples for the Hematological Module of the ABP

5.1 Internal Quality Control Sample Analysis

a) Prior to the analysis of whole blood Samples, the valid lot of the QC samples (e.g., XN CHECK levels 1, 2 and 3), as determined by WADA, shall be analyzed in duplicate. The results shall be in agreement with the relevant ranges established by the manufacturer. If a QC sample result is outside the relevant range, then the relevant QC level shall be analyzed again in duplicate.

b) In analytical sessions including more than thirty (30) Samples (corresponding to approximately sixty (60) measurements), an additional QC sample (either level 1, 2 or 3) shall be inserted and analyzed as a single measurement for each subsequent set of thirty (30) Samples. The additional mid-session QC samples should be incorporated at intervals that ensure an even distribution throughout the analytical sequence. All QC sample measurements shall be in agreement with the relevant ranges established by the manufacturer.

c) At the end of each analysis session, one (1) QC sample (either level 1, 2 or 3) shall be analyzed (only a single measurement is necessary) and meet the specifications provided by the manufacturer to demonstrate the instrument's continued stability.

d) All QC sample results shall be monitored and evaluated via QC-charts to conclude that the results are within the results are within the control limits established by the manufacturer.

5.2 Temperature Data Logger

a) The temperature data logger shall be stopped before the start of the whole blood Sample homogenization procedure, upon removal of the whole blood Sample(s) from the cooling device or refrigerator.

b) In cases when the temperature data logger accompanies multiple whole blood Samples, which are analyzed in the same batch by the Laboratory, the temperature data logger shall be stopped before the homogenization of the first whole blood Sample.

c) The analysis of all whole blood Samples associated with the same temperature data logger shall proceed without delay.

5.3 Whole Blood Sample Analysis and Results Evaluation

a) The whole blood Sample shall be gently homogenized prior to analysis and for at least fifteen (15) minutes using an appropriate mixer (e.g., roller mixer).

b) Each whole blood Sample shall be analyzed in duplicate. The Laboratory's procedure should minimize the delay between the two (2) analyses of each whole blood Sample.

c) Results Acceptance Criteria

Absolute differences between the two (2) analyses shall be equal or less than (≤) each of the following criteria:

i) 0.1 g/dL for HGB.

ii) 0.15% absolute difference for RET% if either the first or second measurement is lower or equal (≤) to 1.00%.

iii) 0.25% absolute difference for RET% if both measurements are greater than (>) 1.00%.

d) The second analysis of a whole blood Sample is used to confirm the results of the first analysis. Therefore:

i) If the absolute differences between the results of the two (2) analyses are within the criteria above, then only the results of the first analysis shall be reported into ADAMS.

  • ii) If the absolute differences between the results of the two (2) analyses are greater than (>) those defined above, then the whole blood Sample shall be analyzed again in accordance with Article 5.3 b).
  • In cases of repeated analysis, the whole blood Sample shall be mixed prior to reanalysis using the automated mixing feature of the blood analyzer or by appropriate manual inversion.
  • This reanalysis procedure shall be repeated until the absolute differences between the results of the two (2) most recent analyses are within the criteria specified above.
  • If the volume of Sample is exhausted before the criteria in 5.3. c) can be met, then the Sample shall be reported as "Not Analyzed" and a description of the issue included as a comment in ADAMS.

e) The requirements for an Initial Testing Procedure (ITP), an "A" Sample Confirmation Procedure (CP) and a "B" Sample CP, as defined in the ISL[1], are not applicable to whole blood Samples analyzed for the Markers of the Hematological Module of the ABP.

6.0 Reporting

6.1 Temperature Data Logger Report

a) The Laboratory shall download and store, for a period of ten (10) years after the reporting of the data in ADAMS, the raw temperature profile report recorded by the temperature data logger.

b) The filename of the raw temperature profile report shall be recorded as the concatenation of the data logger ID with the date of Sample reception by the Laboratory, using the Gregorian calendar, ("YYYY-MM-DD" in local time) separated by an underscore. For example, for a data logger ID "KG34V10" with a Sample reception date of "2027-04-20", the Laboratory shall report the temperature profile under the filename "KG34V10_2027-04-20.txt".

c) The raw temperature profile report recorded by the temperature data logger shall be reported into ADAMS as soon as possible after stopping the temperature data logger. For Samples matched with a temperature data logger, if the Laboratory is unable to upload the temperature profile report into ADAMS, the results of the relevant Sample(s) analysis shall be reported with a comment describing the issue.

6.2 Reporting Whole Blood Sample Results

a) The Laboratory shall report the whole blood Sample analysis results, in ADAMS, as soon as possible after analysis and should be reported within three (3) days after Sample reception.

b) When reporting in ADAMS, the Laboratory shall include, in addition to the mandatory information stipulated in ADAMS, the ISL and relevant ISL TDs:

i) The Laboratory test results (other variables may be included for quality purposes):

ii) Laboratory comment(s) describing any relevant deviation as part of the whole blood Sample's ADAMS record.
