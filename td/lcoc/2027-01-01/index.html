LABORATORY CHAIN OF CUSTODY

1.0 Introduction

The objective of this Technical Document (TD), which constitutes an integral part of the International Standard for Laboratories (ISL)[1], is to harmonize the maintenance by Laboratories and ABP Laboratories (i.e., Laboratories) of Fit-for-purpose Laboratory Chain of Custody (LCOC) procedures, which unequivocally link the analytical results generated by the Laboratory to the Sample and thereby to the Athlete that provided the Sample.

There are two (2) parts involved in the chain of custody for an individual Sample:

i. The external chain of custody initiated at the Sample collection site to record Sample's custody from collection to the delivery to the Laboratory; and

ii. The LCOC from Sample receipt at the Laboratory to its disposal or use for secondary purposes (see ISL Article 5.3.8.2[1]).

2.0 Laboratory Chain of Custody

The LCOC documentation records the chronological traceability of custody (by authorized Person(s) or in defined and controlled storage locations) and the actions performed on the Sample and all Aliquots of the Sample taken for Analytical Testing.

a) The LCOC does not require a separate dedicated form and may include any record (worksheets, logbooks, forms, electronic records, etc.) detailing the custody of Samples and Aliquots.

b) The LCOC shall be a continuous record of authorized individuals and secure storage locations regarding the custody of the Samples or Aliquots.

c) The LCOC is documented by a written or electronic record, which includes the date, location, action(s) taken, and the Person(s) performing an action with a Sample or Aliquot.

d) Any deviations from the LCOC shall be documented as part of the Sample record and include a statement of impact on the Sample's or Aliquot's traceability.

e) Handwritten corrections to hardcopy records shall follow forensic correction principles¹ and shall be conducted in accordance with ISO/IEC 17025.

¹A forensic correction is defined as formally amending inaccurate or incomplete records, while maintaining an audit trail of the original entry. The audit trail of handwritten records is maintained by recording a single strikethrough of the original entry (which ensures it remains legible), recording the new entry, the signature or initials of the individual performing the forensic correction, and the date of the correction.

2.1 Maintaining Laboratory Chain of Custody

a) The Laboratory shall implement LCOC Standard Operating Procedures (SOP) compliant with this ISL TD2027LCOC.

b) The Laboratory shall maintain, as part of its Analytical Testing records, both the Sample external chain of custody documentation and the LCOC.

c) All entries into the LCOC shall be completed by the authorized staff involved at the time that any change of custody occurs (to/from Persons to/from storage devices). Time references do not need to be recorded in the LCOC, except for the time of Sample receipt by the Laboratory.

d) A LCOC is required for both "A" and "B" Sample containers and every Aliquot prepared for an Analytical Testing Procedure (ATP).

e) Samples are considered to be under LCOC when they are:

i. In the possession of authorized Laboratory staff or authorized Person(s); or

[Comment to Article 2.1 e)-i: Authorized Person(s) that are trained, approved by the Laboratory and incorporated within the Laboratory's Management System may be utilized for the reception and transfer of delivered unopened Samples to the Laboratory, for example: facility security personnel.]

ii. Within the secured, restricted and defined controlled zone of the Laboratory for Sample handling; or

iii. Stored in a secured, restricted and defined storage location (within a Laboratory controlled zone or in an external Sample storage facility under the responsibility of the Laboratory - see also ISL Article 5.3.7.2).

f) Aliquots (and resulting extracts) are considered to be under LCOC when they are:

i. In the possession of authorized Laboratory staff; or

ii. Kept in the Laboratory controlled zone (which includes the analytical instrumentation), with access restricted to authorized Laboratory staff only; or

iii. Stored in a secured, restricted and defined Laboratory storage location.

[Comment to Article 2.1: External visitors shall not be present in the Laboratory control zone during an analysis without the supervision of Laboratory staff.[1]]

2.2 Recording Laboratory Chain of Custody during Analytical Testing

The LCOC of Samples shall record all custody from their receipt in the Laboratory, through storage and handling, to Sample disposal or use for secondary purposes (see ISL Article 5.3.8.2 [1]).

a) Sample Reception

The transfer of the Samples from the courier or other delivery Person shall be recorded including:

i. The date of receipt.

ii. The time of receipt.

iii. The initials or (electronic) signature of the authorized Laboratory representative receiving the Sample(s), and

b) Sample Handling

The handling of the Samples during Analytical Testing shall be recorded from reception to disposal/secondary use, including:

i. The date.

ii. The reason/task for the change of the Sample custody.

iii. The Laboratory Sample identification code.

iv. The identity of the Laboratory staff, authorized Person or storage location the Sample is transferred from/to, including the unique equipment identifier of each storage unit (e.g., freezer or refrigerator identification).

c) Aliquot Handling

The LCOC shall record all custody from Aliquot preparation to completion of analysis and disposal including any remaining unused portion of the Aliquot (where applicable), including:

i. The date.

ii. The ATP(s) for which the Aliquot was taken.

iii. The Laboratory Aliquot identification code.

iv. The identity of the authorized Laboratory staff involved in the preparation and transferring of the Aliquot(s) (and the resulting extracts); and

v. The identification of the Laboratory staff or storage location the Aliquot is transferred from/to, including the unique equipment identifier of each storage unit used to store the Aliquot(s) (and resulting extracts).

p. 4

d) Handling Multiple Samples

When multiple Samples are processed, a batch Aliquot LCOC document for Initial Testing Procedures (ITPs) and/or Confirmation Procedures (CPs) may be used in lieu of an individual Aliquot LCOC.

e) Electronic LCOC

i. If all or part of the LCOC is recorded using an electronic data and information management system (e.g., a Laboratory Information Management System, LIMS), then the software shall have the capability to uniquely identify and record the authorized Person(s) involved in the LCOC at the moment that any change of custody occurs.

ii. Electronic LCOC platforms shall have audit trail and data integrity capabilities (e.g., time-stamped entries, user authentication, justified corrections that prevents deletion of records).

iii. In addition, the software shall have the capability to produce a hard copy summary of the Sample and Aliquot LCOC, as described in Article 2.2., for a Laboratory Documentation Package (LDOC).

2.3 Recording Chain of Custody for Sample Transfers

The Laboratory shall incorporate LCOC procedures and forms to record the transfer of Samples and/or Aliquots to another entity (e.g. another Laboratory, WADA-approved laboratory, external long-term storage facility) - see also ISL Article 5.3.7.2.

a) Samples and/or Aliquots may be transported to another Laboratory.

b) The LCOC shall record the removal of the "A" and/or "B" Sample containers from storage, in compliance with Article 2.2 b), including the packaging of Samples and the transfer of custody from the Laboratory to a courier or other responsible shipper.

c) A copy of the above LCOC shall also be included in the shipment and for signature by the recipient of the Samples.
